🔧 How the Earth Jukebox turns NASA pictures into music (for teachers & judges)

Every sound is made live in the browser from the picture itself. The app looks at the pixels of each NASA Earth Information Center (EIC) frame, finds the bright spots (city lights), reads their position, brightness and colour, and turns those numbers into music with the Web Audio API. Notes are kept in a pentatonic scale so the result always sounds pleasant, while the shape, speed and colour of the music come from the data.

What the picture shows (data)What you hear (music)
Height of a light in the picturePitch (higher light = higher note) → melody
Lights in the same columnNotes played together → harmony
Number of lights in a slice of the pictureNote length: lots of lights = sixteenth notes, some = eighths, few = quarters, none = rest → rhythm
Total amount of light in the pictureSpeed of the music → tempo
Colour of a light (red, orange, yellow, green, blue, purple, white)Instrument → timbre
Brightness of a lightVolume and sparkle
Night lights year by year, 2012 → 2023 (total light measured in each EIC frame)More light = more notes, faster tempo, and extra instruments joining in (Earth Time Machine)

How the computer finds the lights: each pixel's brightness is compared with the average of its neighbourhood (local contrast), so cities stand out but large grey areas such as ice stay quiet. The brightest separate points become the "stars" you can tap. Colours are sorted by hue and saturation. Pictures: NASA Earth Information Center night-lights visualization (2012–2023), NASA/ISS crew, NASA Webb, NASA Solar Dynamics Observatory, NASA Chandra.
